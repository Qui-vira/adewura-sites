Let us start by saying that, according to the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC),  Bacterial Vaginosis is not considered to be a sexually transmitted infection. Any woman can get BV, however, it occurs more commonly in women who are sexually active.

A healthy vagina normally contains many “good” bacteria and less “harmful” bacteria. BV is a condition in women where the normal balance of bacteria in the vagina is disrupted and replaced by an overgrowth of “harmful” bacteria which are very diverse, but include Gardnerella vaginalis, Mobiluncus, Bacteroides, and Mycoplasma. Left untreated, it can lead to serious complications like pelvic inflammatory disease, recurrent infections, and even infertility.

What Causes It?

BV is a condition caused by an imbalance of naturally occurring bacterial flora and is often confused with yeast infection (candidiasis), or infection with Trichomonas vaginalis (trichomoniasis), which are not caused by bacteria.  The cause of this condition is still unknown. However, there are some activities and behaviors that can put women at increased risk of getting BV, like changing sex partners or having multiple partners.

What are the symptoms of BV?


Most women with BV do not report having any symptoms at all.  Other women with BV may have an abnormal homogeneous vaginal discharge which is white or grayish in color with an unpleasant fish-like odor, especially after intercourse. Discharge, if present, is usually white or gray; it can be thin. Women with BV may also have burning during urination or itching around the outside of the vagina, or both.
