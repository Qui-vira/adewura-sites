Our custodian

Oba Àtègbè and head diviner, Babaláwo Dr. Ifábùkúnmi O. Adéwálé.

Healer, priest, scholar

Dr. Ifábùkúnmi O. Adéwálé, our Oba Àtègbè, is a practitioner and scholar of traditional Yorùbá medicine and religion. He is the medical director of Adewura Natural Medical Centre in Lagos, Nigeria, and of Adewura Natural Remedies in the United States, and has developed many herbal remedies.

He combines traditional Yorùbá culture with Western academic study, which gives him a wide view across science, culture and health: a doctor of traditional medicine, a Babaláwo, an Oníṣègùn, a pharmacognosist and an ethnobotanist.

Training

Born in Epòró, Emùré-Èkìtì, Èkìtì State, Nigeria, Dr. Adéwálé is an nth-generation Babaláwo and Oníṣègùn, from a long line of healers and priests. Under his father and his uncle, two highly regarded Babaláwo and healers of their time, he trained extensively in divination and healing.

That training kept him from formal schooling after primary school. His curiosity and persistence won him leave to attend college, on one condition: that when his schooling was done, he would return to his spiritual commitments as a practitioner of traditional medicine.

He enrolled at the University of Lagos, where he earned a Bachelor of Science in biology with special honors. He trained as a biosystematic plant taxonomist and carried out a postgraduate research project in pharmacognosy. Together these led to his first major commercial product, Cleanser 109 Mixture. He also holds a postgraduate diploma in computer science.

Teaching and healing

Dr. Adéwálé has given his life to teaching and healing. His botanical garden holds more than a thousand plant species and has become an important teaching site for students of ethnobotany. Over the past decade he has sat on committees of the Lagos State Board of Traditional and Alternative Medicine, and he serves as secretary to the International Congress of Òrìṣà Tradition and Culture.

He has spoken on Yorùbá traditions in forty-two of the fifty United States, and lectured in France, Germany, England, the Caribbean and across Africa. His most recent appointments include posts at the New York Open Center and the University of Georgia.
